# Janhollow

A pixel space station that lives on its own, inside Forge's **Pixel games**
tile. Eight crew with jobs, homes, personalities and daily schedules walk four
decks and take the lift between them. It is built so local Jan models can give
them voices — a 0.8B for crew chatter, a 4B for the narrator — but session 1
calls neither, so what runs today is a station keeping its own time.

Read `MANIFEST.json` first for where the project stands, then
`manifests/roadmap.json` for what comes next.

## Running it

Inside Forge it starts itself: open the **Pixel games** tile and pick
Janhollow. On its own:

```bash
cd ~/Forge/apps/janhollow
FORGE_SHARED_DIR=~/Forge/shared .venv/bin/python -m uvicorn web_app:app --port 8448
```

Then `http://127.0.0.1:8448/`. `#deck=engineering` opens on a deck and
`#pane=settings` on a pane.

Tests: `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests`.

## Where the station lives

Everything the game is made of is JSON in `~/ForgeGameData/janhollow/`:

```
decks/       one file per deck: size, corridors, where the lift opens
locations/   one file per room: which deck, its rectangle, its props
npcs/        one file per crew member: job, home, schedule, relationships
events/      narrator material (session 3 reads these)
quests/      quest shapes (session 5)
saves/       auto.json plus any manual slots
```

## Stepping in

Press **Step aboard** and you arrive as a new arrival in the room with the most
awake crew in it, a step from one of them (the Mess hall when everyone is
asleep). The camera comes in and follows you; step out and it goes back to
showing the whole deck.

Aboard, the deck buttons and **Tab** look at another deck without moving you;
**Back to me** or walking brings the view home. Clicking a name in the Crew pane
finds them: their deck, zoomed in and following them.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WASD** or arrows | walk (free and smooth, and you slide along walls rather than sticking) |
| **Enter**, or just start typing | talk to whoever is within about three tiles |
| **E** or left click | open the menu of what you can do where you stand: sit, sleep, take the lift, talk, pick up, put down, give |
| **1–4** | choose which carried thing is in your hands (a digit still starts talking when that slot is empty) |

The narrator gives you a name and a reason for being aboard shortly after you
arrive — not before, because that is a thirty-second model call and joining
should be instant. Until it lands you are "the new arrival".

Talking to someone warms them to you and they remember what you said, which
feeds back into what they say next. **If Jan is not running they still answer**,
from a short written line chosen by their job: the station never goes mute.

Sleeping on a bunk skips to seven in the morning and leaves the narrator a
summary of the night.

It is one ongoing life, saved with the station.

### Carrying and giving

Things lie about the station — a toolbox in the workshop, a sapling in
hydroponics, a cake in the galley — each with a slow glint so you can find it in
the dark. Walk onto one and pick it up from the menu. You carry up to four, shown
in the play bar; the one outlined there is drawn in your hands. Put it down
anywhere and it stays there, saved. Give it to whoever is beside you: each crew
member has a `likes` list, and a thing they like warms them to you by one (the
same step talking gives, capped at three). Anything else earns a polite thanks.
Only the first gift to a person each station day counts. The rules live in
`janhollow/carry.py`.

## Adding something to carry

One file in `~/ForgeGameData/janhollow/items/`:

```json
{ "id": "teapot", "label": "teapot", "sprite": "teapot", "pack": "janhollow-space",
  "starts_in": ["lounge"], "tags": ["food"] }
```

The sprite must already exist in the named pack — nothing gets invented that
cannot be drawn. A copy appears in each `starts_in` room when a new life starts
(an existing save keeps its floor as you left it). Add the id to a crew member's
`likes` to make it a good gift for them. `tools/seed_items.py` writes the
starting five and the crew's likes.

## Nothing here starts a model on its own

Crew chatter defaults to **off**, and off gates the narrator too, so opening the
game costs nothing. The **Voices** button in the header turns it up in one press
— off, quiet, moderate, lively — and the Settings pane has the same control.
Every Forge app also has a **Close app** button beside Full screen, which stops
its backend rather than merely hiding it.

## The art

Everything on screen is **32 pixels a tile**, in two packs in `~/ForgeArtData`:

- **`janhollow-deck`** — the station itself, generated by
  `tools/make_art32.py`: floor plates with panel seams, bolts and wear, corridor
  grating, a lit strip down the middle of a run, soil for the garden deck, a
  two-panel door, a lift pad, a viewport, and `wall_0`–`wall_15`.
- **`janhollow-crew`** — the eight crew and the furniture, doubled from the 16px
  `crewroom-*` packs with EPX (Scale2x), which rounds a diagonal instead of
  turning one pixel into a 2×2 block. Mixing 16px characters with 32px tiles is
  what actually looks wrong, and this was the cheap way out of it without
  redrawing eight people.

Both are ordinary packs and both are editable in Pixel Forge. Rebuild them with
`.venv/bin/python tools/make_art32.py --force` — the tiles are deterministic, so
a rebuild is byte-identical.

**Walls are autotiled.** A wall picks its variant from which of its four sides
have another wall beside them, so a corner looks like a corner and a face onto a
room gets a lit lip. No file says where a corner is.

**The deck has its own palette** (`janhollow`), separate from the crew's
(`crewroom`), so re-skinning the station — a night shift, a red alert — is one
palette file and no new art. The page bakes one atlas per palette and asks for
sprites by qualified `pack/name`, because the 32px packs reuse the 16px names on
purpose.

**Light** is one dark overlay per deck with holes punched in it at lamps,
consoles, reactors, viewports, doors and the lift, then a warm or cold glow added
back over each. A room lights itself by having a lamp in its `props` — that is a
data edit, not a wiring change.

**Nothing in the code lists what exists.** The types are declared once in
`janhollow/content.py`; the files are found by scanning.

## Adding a crew member

One file, `~/ForgeGameData/janhollow/npcs/ada-keene.json`:

```json
{
  "id": "ada-keene",
  "label": "Ada Keene",
  "job": "Quartermaster",
  "pronouns": "she/her",
  "personality": "Keeps a list of everything and lends nothing twice.",
  "sprite": "person4",
  "home": "quarters_port",
  "relationships": { "dez-halloway": 1 },
  "schedule": [
    { "at": "07:00", "goto": "mess_hall", "activity": "breakfast" },
    { "at": "08:30", "goto": "cargo_bay", "activity": "counting things" },
    { "at": "18:30", "goto": "mess_hall", "activity": "dinner" },
    { "at": "22:00", "goto": "quarters_port", "activity": "sleep" }
  ]
}
```

Press **Re-read content** in the Settings pane (or restart the backend) and she
is aboard. Schedule times must look like `07:00`, must be in order, and must
name a room that exists — a file that breaks those rules is reported in the
Settings pane's Content section instead of breaking the game.

Setting `"aboard": false` retires someone without losing who they were, which
is how crew transferring off the station is meant to work.

## Adding a room

One file in `locations/`, naming its deck and its rectangle in tiles:

```json
{
  "id": "chart_room",
  "label": "Chart room",
  "deck": "command",
  "rect": [23, 15, 8, 8],
  "kind": "work",
  "props": [ { "sprite": "desk", "x": 2, "y": 2 } ]
}
```

Leave at least one tile between rooms and the corridors — that gap becomes the
wall, and the door is cut automatically where the wall meets a corridor. Prop
coordinates are relative to the room's own corner.

## Adding a deck

One file in `decks/` with a size, the corridors to carve and where the lift
opens; then rooms that name it. The four that exist all use the same frame (42
by 25, a corridor crossing, up to eight rooms), but nothing requires that.

## How it fits together

- `janhollow/content.py` — the five content types and their validators.
- `janhollow/station.py` — deck files and room files into walkable decks:
  walls, doors, the lift pad and the pathfinding.
- `janhollow/sim.py` — the clock, the schedules, the walking, the away modes
  and the log. This runs in the backend, not the page, because a station that
  keeps living with the tab closed cannot live in a browser tab.
- `janhollow/saves.py` — the autosave and manual slots.
- `web_app.py` — the HTTP surface, and `forge_jan.mount` for the model
  settings.
- `static/render.js` — drawing one deck; `static/game.js` — polling, the
  panels and the settings.

The page never decides where anyone goes. It polls `/api/world/state` three
times a second, eases each crew member towards where the server says they are,
and sends a heartbeat to `/api/world/presence` — which is the only way the
backend can tell "you are watching" from "the tab is gone", and so the only
reason the away modes work.

## The away modes

Set per world in the Settings pane; the default is the first.

| Mode | What it does |
|---|---|
| Pause when unfocused | The station holds still the moment you look away. |
| Keep running when closed | Life aboard carries on, and (from session 2) the crew keep talking, with the tab shut. |
| Idle when closed | The crew keep to their day with the tab shut, but no model runs until you return. |
| Time-skip summary | Nothing runs while you are away; on your return the station jumps ahead — capped at twelve station hours — and leaves a summary for the narrator to write. |

## Jan, and the voices

Two roles, picked in the Settings pane and preferred in `janhollow/roles.py`:

| Role | Model | Where | Cost, measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chatter` | Qwen3.5-0.8B | CPU | 0.6–0.9 s a line |
| `narrator` | Qwen3.5-4B | iGPU | 21–34 s a paragraph |

**Thinking has to be off.** Qwen3.5 reasons by default, and asked for one line
of dialogue it spends every token reasoning and returns nothing: 5.7 s and an
empty reply, against 0.9 s and the line with
`chat_template_kwargs: {enable_thinking: false}`. `janclient.py` sends that on
every request.

**Name your models.** With no setting, `shared/forge_jan.py` falls back to
whatever Jan lists first — which here was an unrelated 2B that leaked thinking
into the crew's mouths. `roles.py` is this app's preference; your own choice in
the Jan panel still wins over it.

**One request at a time.** There is a single lane in `janclient.py`, because two
concurrent Jan requests once froze this laptop. The chatter would rather skip a
turn than queue behind the narrator, so it waits three seconds for the lane and
then gives up.

Jan is shared with other Forge apps and is never started, stopped or
reconfigured by this one.

### The quality guard

the owner's condition for running local models at all was "as long as the outputs
are fine", so nothing a model says reaches the screen unchecked. `guard.py`
holds ten rules in a registry — empty, no letters, too long, not English,
narration, name label, talking about itself in the third person, echoing,
parroting the last line, and looping — each one a pure function, each one added
because the 0.8B actually did it. A rejected line is retried once; if the retry
also fails the conversation simply ends, and the sample is written to
`~/ForgeGameData/janhollow/rejected.jsonl` (capped at 200). That file is the
record of how this model fails and is the right place to start before tuning a
prompt.

Adding a rule is one entry in `CHECKS`.

### Who talks, and when

`director.py` is the only thing in the app that calls Jan, on a thread of its
own so the ten-times-a-second simulation never waits for a model. Its order is:
a time-skip that is waiting for its paragraph, then a station day that has not
been narrated, then a conversation if one is due. The gap between conversations
comes from the Crew chatter setting — Off, Quiet, Lively or Moderate, which is
about one a minute — and `world.models_allowed()` can veto all of it, which is
how "Idle when closed" keeps the crew walking their day in silence until you
come back.

Two buttons in the Settings pane do it on demand: **Make someone talk** and
**Narrate today**.

### Written lines: small talk without Jan

With Voices off, or while Jan is not answering, crew who share a room still
pass the time in **written lines**: an opener from one and, usually, an answer
from the other, chosen to fit the speaker's job, the room and the time of day.
They greet you in passing the same way. Nobody who is asleep says anything,
and no model runs for any of it. Settings → **Written lines** sets how often:
Off, Now and then (about one a minute, the default) or Often.

The lines are data in `barks/`, one JSON file per set (the format is in the
docstring at the top of `janhollow/barks.py`; `barks/cook.json` is a short
example). A new set is one new file in `~/ForgeGameData/janhollow/barks/`; a
file there with the same name as a shipped one replaces it. A broken file is
skipped and named under Settings, and the rest still load.

### The log

The Log pane has four filters above it — Everything, Talk, Stories, and
Comings and goings — and, while a crew member is picked in the Crew pane, an
"Only <name>" chip for just their lines. The choice is remembered. New lines
are added at the end without rebuilding the pane, so a line you have scrolled
up to read stays put; at the bottom the pane follows the newest line. The
filters are data: one entry in `FILTERS` in `static/logview.js`.

### What the dialogue is actually like

Short, in character and station-appropriate, and sometimes a little odd — it is
a 416 MB model. It is never garbage: no other languages, no stage directions,
no JSON, no parroting. If you want better lines, change `prefer` in
`roles.py`; Jan also has a 2B, a 4B and a 9B, at a cost in seconds per line.
