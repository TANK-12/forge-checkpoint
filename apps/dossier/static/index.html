Keep the library current

News is gathered per subject when that subject is researched, so a library left alone slowly goes stale and the briefing quietly describes an older world. The watcher closes that loop: on a timer it queues another quick pass over whichever subjects have gone longest without one, and the ordinary merge rules fold in whatever is new. Nothing already held is lost or duplicated.
reading…
It never clears a pause, never pushes past a queue that is still working, and never overrides the memory guard — so a bulk run you started always takes precedence, and a sweep that has to wait is simply retried later.

Get all the new info

Gives every subject already held the past five years of news and its full encyclopedia article. The news is Wikipedia's daily Current Events digest — sixty month pages, downloaded once and shared by the whole library — and an item belongs to a subject because it links that subject's article, not because a name looked similar. Every item keeps the outlets it cites. New research passes do this on their own; this catches up what was researched before. No model is used. Your old data is kept: news merges in without replacing anything, and a subject that already has its article keeps it. Each dossier also has its own Get new info button.

into the active queue
Subjects are queued most-covered-first, measured by how many language Wikipedias carry an article on them. A job stopped halfway has still done the consequential ones. The queue is on disk, so closing the app costs at most the subject in flight — a pause is still a pause tomorrow morning. Subjects already in the library are skipped unless the queue is set to redo them.

Queues

Off, the active queue skips any subject already in the library and marks it skipped rather than researching it twice. On, it researches everything again — which is what you want after the pipeline learns to collect something new.
